# Every Opening Act on Ed Sheeran's Tour Walked Away

**What happened** · Artists participating in Ed Sheeran's world tour have exited the tour in solidarity with Macklemore's pro-Palestinian comments. The Jakarta Post reported on September 16, 2026.

---

By September 15, every opening act on Ed Sheeran's world tour had walked away. Aaron Lowe, FINNEAS and Lukas Graham left one after another, and Beoga, the band travelling with the tour, went too, according to reports in Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter ([The Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/culture/2026/09/16/artists-exit-sheeran-tour-in-solidarity-with-macklemore-pro-palestinian-comments), CBC News). What set it off was the removal of Macklemore, who chanted "Free Palestine" and performed "Hind's Hall" at the MetLife Stadium shows on September 4 and 5, as Variety reported. An opening act is the artist who plays before the headliner, usually paid far less than the main name and compensated mostly in exposure. It is also the easiest slot on any tour to cut.

The party that changed the lineup was not the one renting the building but the one that owns it. The side of Robert Kraft, owner of Gillette Stadium, said it would not give its stage to hate speech, PBS NewsHour reported. Sheeran said several venues had told him they would cancel the show if Macklemore stayed on the bill, according to Variety. Macklemore has claimed that Kraft pulled other venue owners together into an ultimatum, CBC News reported. The explanations differ, but the place where the decision was made does not. Once tickets are sold, a venue refusing to open its doors is no longer just a penalty clause. It becomes leverage.

That also explains why the resistance came from below the headliner rather than from him. The promoter, the company that books and finances the tour dates, said that cancelling the tour would affect hundreds of thousands of people, [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/macklemore-dropped-ed-sheeran-tour-pro-palestine-statements-1235625967/) reported. Sheeran apologised at the Philadelphia show on the 19th and the tour continued, according to The Washington Post, and he reportedly wept on stage, per Global News. The person carrying the cancellation cost and the people losing a support slot were never choosing between the same options.

My bet: within the next 180 days, at least one more North American stadium or arena tour will have a support act dropped or swapped at the demand of a venue owner or promoter, and the speech clauses buried in support-act contracts will become a subject of open industry argument. I will check it against drop-out coverage in Billboard, Variety or Pollstar, and against public statements from artist management, venue operating companies or unions.

For Korean acts moving into North American arenas, two lines in the contract matter before anything else. Who holds approval over guest and opening slots, and whether the venue rental agreement allows cancellation over what someone says on stage. If your business runs on fans bonding with an artist's stated values, you need to know at signing whether the stage where those values get spoken belongs to someone else.

Any business that distributes through space it does not own, from a brand inside a department store to a studio releasing through a platform, inherits that owner's limits on what it is allowed to say.

**Prediction** · Within 180 days, at least one more North American stadium/arena tour will see a support act dropped or replaced at a venue owner's or promoter's demand, and speech clauses in support-act contracts will surface as a publicly discussed industry issue · within 180 days · check: At least one drop-out report in Billboard/Variety/Pollstar, plus public statements from artist management, venue operators, or unions about contract speech clauses

**Takeaway** · If you rent your venue, you don't own your lineup - whoever owns the distribution space ultimately holds final say over what goes in it.
