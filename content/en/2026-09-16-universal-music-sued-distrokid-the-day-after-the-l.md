IFPI launched the Streaming Integrity Initiative and handed distributors a five-point list: rights and identity verification, content screening, fraud response, information sharing between distributors, and stronger defensive systems. The sequence matters more than either event. When Sony went after Trefuego, [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/on-distrokid-trefuego-modified-audio-and-the-potential-for-a-new-napster-nightmare/) noted that DistroKid was not a defendant and carried only a royalty-sharing consequence. This time the distributor is in the dock. Liability moved one layer up from the uploader, and signing the initiative has become the way to step out of that seat. [Digital Music News](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/distrokid-which-distributes-up-to-40-of-all-new-music-hasnt-signed-ifpis-new-anti-fraud-standards-unlike-cd-baby/) reportedly observed that at least one participating company had previously handled unauthorized remixes and mass-produced AI tracks, and appears to have avoided litigation through the pact. That reads less like a certificate and more like litigation insurance.

Screening standards will not arrive everywhere at the same speed. For a distributor without review staff and a clean metadata system, the five points are a compliance bill. For one that has both, they are a filter against competitors. [MediaNama](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMifkFVX3lxTE4xbzVibUtqWmJuZWdfejRLUS1LR1hqOGtXQ1ZBb0lOWFZQeHZ0TmVVQU45dlFMaHhvS0ZhM20wNXhjdDdxZjh1Nm9BU1BKNmR4dDlhNmozVHVvcW94aUNOVk5rTHlJNHVVSU1NdFFYazV3MGxuOW5tb21YQmxMZw?oc=5) reported that Indian distributors have not joined. Deezer's own newsroom counted up to 85% of fully AI-generated tracks in 2025 as fraudulent streams, up from an initial figure of around 70%, and that arithmetic is now being pushed from the platform down to the distribution layer.

My call: within 180 days, at least one major Korean distributor among Dreamus, Kakao Entertainment and NHN Bugs will write AI-track identity and rights verification, or an AI labeling rule, into its public upload terms or artist submission guide. I will check it against a terms revision notice from the company, or a Korean name appearing on the SII signatory list. Where I could be wrong: Korean distributors may absorb the demands of their overseas distribution partners as internal process and simply never document them publicly.

If you are reviewing a contract in this space, two lines carry the weight. First, when a track slips past screening, whether the residual liability sits with the distributor or the uploader. Second, how far the information-sharing clause lets your artists' identity and settlement data travel to competing distributors. Any marketplace that has been absorbing unlimited third-party supply, from app stores to e-commerce platforms, should assume its own verification duty will land on the intermediary rather than the seller.
