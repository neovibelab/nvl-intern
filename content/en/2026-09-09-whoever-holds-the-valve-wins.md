Suno announced new per tier download caps for its free and paid users and said it will watermark AI generated tracks, according to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/suno-unveils-download-caps-for-free-paid-tiers-generator-1236831589/). The move implements part of its settlement with Warner Music, while suits from Universal Music Group, Sony Music and SOCAN remain live. Downloads, though, are only one of several exits an AI generation can take. Free tier users were never the commercial distributors in the first place, and no figure has been published for how many tracks the higher paid tiers actually allow. A plain reading works just as well: GPU costs are real, and metered downloads are a standard upsell design. What is verifiable is not that less music will leave the tool, but that a countable number now exists where none did.

Comparisons to YouTube's Content ID overstate the resemblance. [Content ID](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370?hl=en) identifies and splits revenue after the fact; a download cap restricts volume before the fact. The two run in opposite directions. What they share is the outcome that rightsholders now have a unit of measurement sitting inside the operator's own systems. Pricing training data takes years of litigation. Counting daily downloads takes a dashboard. Watermarks are reportedly removable through re encoding or pitch shifting in many documented cases, which would make them weak as the sole basis for a compliance decision. And only one of the four major parties has settled, which is evidence this is a deal, not a standard.

AI disclosure itself is already moving. Spotify has reportedly introduced AI music spam filtering and an AI involvement disclosure field with support for DDEX standards, and DistroKid and CD Baby ask uploaders whether AI was used. My bet sits one layer above that. Within 180 days, at least one Korean music distributor or chart body will put in writing that missing AI disclosure or failed provenance verification is grounds for rejecting a submission or excluding a track from charts. Voluntary self reporting and mandatory refusal are different instruments.

Korean precedent supports this less than I would like. In 2021 Melon (멜론), operated by Kakao Entertainment (카카오엔터테인먼트), scrapped its real time chart for 24 hour cumulative counting, and Genie Music (지니뮤직) and FLO (플로) adjusted too. But all of that touched how plays are counted, not who qualifies to submit. Playback patterns are a platform's own server data. Production provenance is information copied down from an outsider. If I am wrong, I will say so here.

For K pop producers, the asset worth building is documentation rather than scarcity. Whoever already holds disclosure records and production logs will concede less in overseas distribution talks.

Any industry facing incoming rules should note that the party who builds the counter, not the party who obeys it, writes the next contract.
