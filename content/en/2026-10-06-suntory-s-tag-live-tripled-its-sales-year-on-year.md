# Suntory's TAG-LIVE! Tripled Its Sales Year on Year

**What happened** · Nikkei Cross Trend reported a case where a beverage vending machine was converted into an 'oshikatsu' content-linked sales device. The vending machine recorded sales increases of more than threefold, demonstrating how fandom consumption is expanding into non-entertainment sectors including food, beverages, and convenience retail.

- **Sutopuri** · Japanese six-member entertainment group founded in June 2016, primarily active on video-sharing platforms like YouTube and Nico Nico Douga. The online singing group that Suntory BF partnered with for a vending machine collaboration in June 2026.
- **DyDo Group Holdings** · Japanese beverage manufacturer and distributor founded in 1975. The company that sold 50 million cans of Demon Slayer tie-up coffee in three weeks in 2021 but later cut its vending machine fleet.
- **Sapporo Holdings** · Japanese beverage and food company engaged in beer, food products, and real estate leasing operations. Founded in 1876. The company that sold a vending operation of roughly 40,000 machines around early 2026.

**Why this one** · It challenges the assumption that fan touchpoints require renting retail space. If a single machine can become a merch outlet, the math behind tour routing, pop-up placement, and inventory allocation changes.

**What is showing** · While Suntory BF expanded TAG-LIVE! into a three-service lineup with on-the-spot acrylic card printing, DyDo is cutting 20,000 machines and Sapporo sold off some 40,000 - the same infrastructure emptying out as one player repurposes it.

---

Suntory Beverage & Food, the drinks arm of the Japanese brewer, says its vending machine business TAG-LIVE! tripled its sales year on year, according to [Nikkei Cross Trend](https://xtrend.nikkei.com/atcl/contents/18/01480/). The machines sell oshikatsu (推し活), a Japanese word for the money and time people spend supporting a favourite singer, group or character, a market reportedly worth 3.4 trillion yen. Suntory BF has set targets of several billion yen within 2026 and more than 10 billion yen by 2028. On 17 June 2026 it launched a machine tied to the online singing group Sutopuri (Strawberry Prince (すとぷり)) that plays voice clips and songs, and added a service that prints an acrylic collectible card on the spot, bringing the line to three services, Nikkei Cross Trend reported.

The company's own explanation is the whole business. Merchandise selling is concentrated in big cities and carries inventory risk, and a vending machine network already standing across the country is a physical counter that solves both. Fan spending tends to swell once someone moves past simply liking a group and starts buying goods and concert tickets. If the only places to collect those goods are Tokyo and Osaka, part of the audience never enters that stage. The constraint sits on the handover side, not the production side.

The same hardware has been used the other way before. DyDo (Daido (ダイド)ー) sold a cumulative 50 million cans of its Demon Slayer tie-up coffee within three weeks of release in 2021, and its operating profit reached 1.5 times the year-earlier figure, Anime News Network reported, citing Mainichi. That structure borrowed an intellectual property to sell more beverage, and numbers lifted by a title end when the campaign ends. The machine was a better-selling shelf for a season, and never gave fans a reason to come back. DyDo Group Holdings announced in February 2026 that it would cut 20,000 of its 270,000 domestic machines and posted a net loss of 30.3 billion yen for the year to January 2026, Newsis reported. Around the same period Sapporo Holdings sold a vending operation of roughly 40,000 machines.

What Suntory BF holds is a condition rather than a clever design. Building a nationwide network means paying for equipment first, so only an operator whose machines are already installed can use this. The rights holder sends a digital file and cards are printed only as they sell, so it carries no stock while multiplying collection points nationwide. The machine owner raises revenue per unit on an asset whose beverage demand has faded. The exchange holds only while enough of the network survives. If the cuts continue and the surviving locations drift away from where fans actually walk, the word nationwide loses its value before anything else does. Any business sitting on installed physical capacity with collapsing core demand owns a distribution asset before it owns a problem.

**In short** · Suntory BF is using machines it already paid to install to hand fans printed cards and music, and that works while enough of those machines stay where fans walk.
