# Can a Promoter Cover the Rent on 880-Yen Fortunes?

**What happened** · Hololive VTuber Sakura Miko (さくらみこ) and LED TOKYO will hold a collaborative experience event at Decks Tokyo Beach 3F from October 15 to November 1. The event features large LED screen videos, original merchandise with new illustrations, and special items like omikuji and mystery rallies.

- **LED TOKYO** · A Japanese LED display manufacturer and rental company. Founded in 2015, with CEO Naoki Suzuki. the installation firm running the collaboration event with Sakura Miko.
- **Cover Corp** · <cite index="13-7,17-8">Japanese company founded in June 2016</cite> that <cite index="7-2">owns hololive production VTuber agency</cite>. the agency operating hololive.
- **Sakura Miko** · Japanese VTuber affiliated with Hololive Production, debuted on August 1, 2018. Has over 2 million YouTube subscribers. the hololive VTuber running the collaboration event.

**Why this one** · When a VTuber IP rents real retail space for a ticketed experience rather than just selling goods, the unit economics of fandom business shift from distribution margin to floor rent and turnover.

**What is showing** · This is the third experiential format cycling through the same venue (LED DAIBA STUDIO), after the July-August 'Tokyo Rainbow Summer Festival' and LED sponsorship of VTUBER EXPO 2026 in May. A display-hardware vendor turning into an IP experience operator is not a one-off here.

---

A Japanese virtual performer is spending eighteen days inside a shopping complex on Tokyo Bay. [Real Sound (リアルサウンド)](https://realsound.jp/tech/2026/10/post-2542450.html) reports that Sakura Miko, a VTuber signed to hololive, runs a collaboration event with the installation firm LED TOKYO from 15 October to 1 November 2026 on the third floor of DECKS Tokyo Beach in Odaiba (お台場). A VTuber is a streamer who performs as an animated character rather than on camera; hololive is the agency that manages her, operated by Cover Corp. Admission is free.

Free admission normally files an event under marketing. A brand rents a room, shows the character, sells some merchandise, books the cost against promotion. This one moves the till away from the door and spreads it through the time visitors spend inside. Real Sound reports that omikuji, the paper fortune slips sold at Japanese shrines, go for 880 yen on site, and that a clue-collecting puzzle rally is also a paid activity. Every 3,000 yen of merchandise earns a postcard, and Koubo reports that a fan-meeting lottery was added, enterable only through the QR code printed on the back of that postcard. A visitor who walked in for nothing leaves behind an activity fee, a merchandise receipt and an entry form.

The press release shows who designed this. The release on [PR TIMES](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000040.000098528.html) was filed by LED TOKYO, headed by Suzuki Naoki, with an IP event and merchandise company reportedly named alongside it. The venue, LED DAIBA STUDIO, is a room LED TOKYO operates itself. The same company supplied large LED screens as a sponsor at VTUBER EXPO 2026 in May 2026, and ran a rainbow-themed summer festival in the same room in July and August 2026, also free to enter, according to its own notices. The box stays; the character inside it gets swapped.

Charging nothing at the door removes the usual tool for managing crowds. Operators said entry may be restricted when the floor gets busy, per the release carried by zaikei. A queue does the work a ticket price would have done.

The party holding a room with fixed monthly costs has a reason to borrow someone else's character: it raises occupancy. When the character arrives, the same floor area works as a live demonstration of the screens LED TOKYO sells and as a place where fans spend money, at once. Waiving admission raises the headcount, and that headcount becomes evidence in the installation business's sales deck. The arithmetic holds only while the company keeps owning the space and repeating the format, spreading fixed costs across many tenancies. A promoter renting someone else's floor for eighteen days cannot cover the rent on 880-yen fortunes and merchandise sold in 3,000-yen increments.

Any business sitting on underused square footage, from department stores to cinema lobbies, is looking at the same trade: lend the room to someone else's audience, and charge for the minutes rather than the entrance.
