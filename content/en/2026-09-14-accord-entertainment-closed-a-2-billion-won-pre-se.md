**What is showing** · Blast's 2023 KRW 2bn pre-Series A and Accord's 2026 round share the same 'not tech, but IP' pitch, while established agencies (Abyss's Miwan Sonyeon (미완소년), Dnable (디네이블)'s Bigrits) launch virtual boy groups in the same quarter.

Accord Entertainment, the company behind the virtual boy group Miracle, has closed a 2 billion won pre-Series A from Smilegate, CNTTECH and SM Culture Partners, as reported by [Edaily](https://edaily.co.kr/News/Read?mediaCodeNo=257&newsId=05120086645579136). The stated use of proceeds covers production systems, owned IP, new content, global fandom and a next virtual project.

The obvious objection is that this sentence is three years old. In March 2023, Blast (VLAST (블래스트)), the agency behind Plave (PLAVE (플레이브)), announced a pre-Series A of the same size with the same round label, and Platum reported at the time that the money was going toward accelerating IP. Press releases in this category have said "IP" for years. On wording alone, nothing has moved.

What has moved is what sits immediately behind the announcement. Miracle added a fifth member, Jiwoon (Jiun (지운)), and released a choreography video for "Seotun Hanghaeilji" (서툰 항해일지) as the group's first five-person performance, with the company signalling that the five-member narrative will be written into the next single, according to Top Celeb News. Since debuting on 26 January 2026, the group has run a monthly release cadence under the banner Miracle The Series, as reported by Shina Ilbo, and Electronic Times reported that the seventh single "Kkum-ui Seonjang" (꿈의 선장) arrived on 30 July. Adding a member is a public stress test of whether the fictional world has an empty seat in it. Existing fans have already settled into a four-person story, while a new member restarts the relationship from the first contact. Monthly releases are the mechanism for closing that gap quickly. When CEO Choi Ji-i (Choi Jii (최지이)) told [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2026/09/01/2026090111074714668) that virtual content is ultimately an IP business rather than a technology business, this roster expansion is the operational content of that claim.

My bet is that within 180 days at least two more Korean virtual-idol agencies will raise outside capital, and their announcements will lead with worldbuilding, serialized content and overseas fandom rather than rendering pipelines or engine work. I will check this against the venture databases TheVC and Wowtale, and against the use-of-proceeds language in each company's own release. The candidate pool is already there, since established agencies keep launching virtual boy groups, including Abyss Company (어비스컴퍼니)'s (Abyss Company) Miwansonyeon (Miwan Sonyeon) and Dnable's (Dnable) Bigrits (Bigritz (비그릿츠)).

Anyone planning a lineup change in a virtual act has one thing to verify first. Go back to the songs and videos released before the expansion and ask whether they now read as the prequel to the new lineup. If they do not, the change is a reboot rather than an expansion, and the item requiring an explanation to investors is not the new member but the back catalogue being written off.

Serialized game studios face the same audit when they add a playable character mid-franchise, and the question investors should ask there is identical: does the existing content still count.
