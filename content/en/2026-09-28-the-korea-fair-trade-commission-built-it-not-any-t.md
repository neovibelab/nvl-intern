# The Korea Fair Trade Commission Built It, Not Any Talent Agency

**What happened** · Law firm Lin (린) launched a dedicated entertainment team. The legal service demands in K-content industry expanded from exclusive contracts to M&A, management rights, and format license revenue distribution by country. AI-generated virtual characters and artists' direct IP utilization emerged as new legal issues.

- **Lin** · South Korean law firm based in Seoul that merged with Tech & Law LLC in 2019. Law firm that launched a dedicated entertainment practice.
- **Korea Fair Trade Commission** · South Korea's regulatory authority for economic competition established in 1981. Regulator that amended and enforced guidelines on endorsement advertising.
- **Review Guideline on Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising** · U.S. FTC guidelines ensuring endorsements and testimonials in advertising are truthful and not misleading. Revised in 2023 to address social media influencers and fake reviews. Guideline that took effect June 1, 2026, adding virtual persons as recognized endorser categories.

**Why this one** · A Korean law firm that used to handle exclusive-contract disputes has bundled M&A, format-license revenue splits, and AI-generated lookalikes into one team. Where legal demand moves shows where entertainment companies' next businesses are headed.

**What is showing** · Korea's FTC added a fifth endorser category, 'virtual persona,' to its advertising review guidelines effective June 1, and multiple law firms are simultaneously spinning up advisory lines on the same issue.

---

A Korean law firm called Lin has launched a dedicated entertainment practice, [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026092775371) reported. The team's head, Do Hyun-soo (Do Hyeonsu (도현수)), told [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20260918000281) on September 18 that the entertainment business is growing complex in its structures and legal risks at the same rate it is growing in size, and described solutions staged by business phase. Among the new disputes the coverage lists is one that did not exist a few years ago: an AI-generated virtual person built to resemble a specific celebrity, then used in advertising.

The fence around that already exists. It was built by the Korea Fair Trade Commission, the country's competition and consumer protection regulator, not by any talent agency. An amended review guideline on endorsements and testimonials in advertising took effect on June 1, 2026, according to a Kim & Chang newsletter and the outlet Ilgan NTN. A review guideline is a document in which the regulator writes down in advance what it will treat as a violation. It sits below statute but functions like a rulebook in practice. The amendment expanded the recognized categories of endorser from four, meaning consumers, celebrities, experts and organizations, to five, adding virtual persons, as summarized in a newsletter from the firm Hwawoo (화우). A violation counts as deceptive advertising, exposing the advertiser to corrective orders, fines and criminal liability. Separately, the AI Framework Act that took effect in January 2026 caps administrative fines for failing to label AI-generated output at 30 million won, according to the firm Minhoo (Minwho (민후)).

Read the structure of that rule. The protected party is the consumer. The regulated party is the advertiser. The artist whose face was borrowed appears nowhere in the text as a party to anything. What the artist gets is a side effect of someone else's protection.

The limits follow from the same structure. Attach a line saying "virtual person generated by AI" and the labeling duty is satisfied, the consumer is no longer deceived, and the question of whose face it resembles is untouched. A disclosure rule can end up mapping the safe zone for imitation. Resemblance fights still have to be run separately, through publicity rights, meaning control over the commercial use of one's own name and likeness, and through unfair competition law.

There is still a use for it. Judging resemblance takes expert assessment and time. Judging whether a label is present takes a glance at the screen. Adding that one check to monitoring costs almost nothing, and a complaint can be filed without the artist stepping forward. Exclusive contracts, the agreements under which an agency manages an artist's work, increasingly carry clauses on face and voice data. Writing in the agency's authority to file and respond on the artist's behalf opens this channel.

Lin's launch also reads as a bid for market share. The firm Gwangjang (광장) published on AI virtual person advertising rules earlier, and Herald Economy (Herald Business (헤럴드경제)) reported that law firms are accelerating entertainment team buildouts. The contested ground moved from contracts to outputs.

Any business whose brand assets can be approximated rather than copied should read the consumer protection rules of the markets where the approximation gets sold, because that is where its first precedent is being written.

**Takeaway** · The fence around an asset is often built first by the regulator of the market where it gets misused, not by the asset's owner-so the first precedent for your rights gets written in a room you weren't in.
