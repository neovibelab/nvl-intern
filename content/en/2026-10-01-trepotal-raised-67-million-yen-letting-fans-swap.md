# Trepotal Raised 67 Million Yen Letting Fans Swap

**What happened** · Japanese fan goods trading platform Toreportal raised approximately 6.7 billion yen in pre-Series A funding. Secondary market structuring for oshikatsu-related goods is underway.

- **streamart** · A merchandise sales service for VTubers. Japan-based streamart sells digital and physical goods via gacha mechanics and allows fans to display purchased items on their "oshi shelf". A streamer merchandise online store that opened an official link with Trepotal.

**Why this one** · As fan-goods resale shifts from person-to-person trading to a funded platform business, rights holders who once sold merch only once now have to design for the data and fees generated in the resale layer.

**What is showing** · Fan-to-fan goods swapping, long run informally over X DMs, is being formalized into search, templated messaging and anonymous shipping-1,000+ completed swaps, adoption by Japan's MIC ICT Startup League, and an August 2026 tie-up with streamer-merch store streamart show third parties filling a slot official rights holders cannot enter.

---

A Japanese app called Trepotal (トレポータル), which lets fans swap merchandise with each other, announced on 29 September 2026 that it had raised about 67 million yen in a pre-Series A round. The money had actually arrived in August, through a third-party share allotment, with the Chiba Dojo Fund leading and two individual investors reportedly joining. A pre-Series A is the small round a startup takes once the product demonstrably works but before it tries to grow fast. Converted, this is a few hundred thousand dollars. On size alone it does not belong in entertainment news.

What makes it worth reading is where the goods come from. A large share of official pop merchandise, the photo cards and small acrylic figures sold at concerts and online, is sold blind. You pay, and only then find out which member of the group you got. That randomness is the mechanism that makes one fan buy the same item six times. The cost of the mechanism is that every fan ends up holding items of members they did not want. The surplus is not a defect in the system. It is the system working.

And the rights holder cannot clear that surplus. The moment a label offers to swap your duplicate for the one you wanted, blind packaging stops doing its job. So the swapping went somewhere else. As [BRIDGE](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiiAFBVV95cUxNVmswS0d2dEk1TkFDM3JabE5XVm56UGlua2lES0pKZDduV2VsVWtFMWpmMXBDY0pQTndtUEIzUnhiWnhVVlNlbVJBTUlRbnl6VzBWaE5jVTNSdERWU05HUUFqVUl4OUJjcVRMM2wxUEFvZmxsMDRHNzNxSmZTWmZub2oyYlpOMTh5?oc=5) described, these trades ran informally on X, formerly Twitter, for years, and Trepotal simply added search, standard message templates and anonymous shipping on top of that habit. On 31 August 2026, just before the round was announced, the app opened an official link with streamart, an online store selling streamer merchandise, so that goods bought there can be managed and traded inside Trepotal. [Anime Anime (アニメ！アニメ！)](https://animeanime.jp/article/2026/08/31/102551.html) reported the tie-up. That is the point where an unofficial resale layer started getting plumbed into an official sales channel.

The weakness is visible too. Barter has no payment in it, so there is no transaction to take a cut from. More trades mean more support costs and no more revenue, which is why the route to money runs through partnerships like streamart, toward the shops and content owners themselves. The size of the round reads like a company at exactly that stage.

For a Korean label, the asset here is the trade log. Which member's card piles up unwanted and which one is scarce is a direct map of demand across a merchandise line, and the basis for next season's packing ratios and order volumes. That map is currently being drawn inside someone else's app.

Any manufacturer that sells assortments it cannot return should note that the resale board is where its demand data now lives.

**Takeaway** · Policing the resale of user-held surplus costs more than brokering it-open the slot you cannot legally occupy to a third party and buy the data back.
