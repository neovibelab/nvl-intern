# Partisan Bought Firebird's Stake in OTM, and Folded In Its Own Left Music

**What happened** · Partisan acquired a stake in OTM Music, an independent publishing company of Firebird, and integrated it with Left Music. Independent labels are consolidating catalogs to strengthen distribution negotiating power.

**Why this one** · Partisan is buying into indie publisher OTM Music and folding it into Left Music. Independent labels that grew on recordings and distribution are now reordering their expansion to own the publishing side of the song.

**What is showing** · Partisan moved from a Sony admin deal (2022) to owning its own publishing vehicle, and on the same day Ace Records signed its first-ever physical distribution deal with an independent UK firm rather than a major - two indies reclaiming their own channel in a single day.

---

Partisan Records has bought out Firebird's stake in OTM Music, an independent publishing company, and will fold it together with Left Music, Partisan's own publishing arm, [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/partisan-acquires-firebirds-interest-in-indie-pubco-otm-music-merges-it-with-its-left-music-publishing-arm/) reported. The price was not disclosed, according to [Music Week](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi2gFBVV95cUxOXzNaNWZoamFPVDg1MkhUS2VRUDhUNTExZ2NncXY2dUgwVGlPc2k4QU9QblM0bHFKT3BoMFFicHNmUVo2MHFjRHRYdVJYME1UZVNfdHcxY2tMcGdMY0ZQeURxOVVzRm90b2VGMDZMZE9oZ3VnajNMZlc1N3gzQXg0dE44ajYzSy1ubXc5WnJaMFpYVnBsVnJmR05idE9VWHRIeWd6NmktUTJyOXktSGVvZUVlTjNmdWRNUXRCSldDck9MbF9WZE5nb0JDS2MtUUdCLU9aUHpWYlV1dw?oc=5). Publishing is the side of the business that handles rights in the song itself, the composition and the lyrics, as distinct from rights in a particular recording of it.

The detail worth holding onto is that the buyer's name disappears. Music Week reports that Left's team and its roster move into OTM, and OTM becomes the group's main publishing entity. The reason shows up in a Digital Music News report: Partisan's sync team and OTM's sync team are being merged into a single global sync service that sells the group's entire repertoire from one place. A sync licence is permission to put a song into a film, a series, an advertisement or a game, and it is negotiated deal by deal on short deadlines. The music supervisor who has to clear a scene by Friday wants one list and one person to price it. So the substance of this deal is not that the catalogue got bigger. It is that the number of doors leading to that catalogue went from two to one.

Left launched in March 2022 with a global administration deal with Sony Music Publishing UK, as Music Week and Music Business Worldwide reported at the time. Administration means the major handles registration, collection and accounting of royalties on Left's behalf. That was renting a counter inside someone else's building. This merger pulls one counter, the sync counter, back in house.

Pulling everything in house is not automatically right. The same week, the UK reissue label Ace Records handed its physical distribution, the business of getting CDs and vinyl into shops, to an outside firm called Proper for the first time in its history, Music Ally reported. Where volume sets the unit price, you rent. Where relationships and the shape of your list set the price, you hold. Leaving administration with Sony while merging the sync desks is the same calculation run twice.

Korean production companies have a version of this problem sitting in plain sight. If the person handling sync on the recorded music side and the person handling sync on the publishing side each farm out deals to different outside agencies, the same drama's music supervisor receives two quotes from what is nominally one company, at two different prices, and learns to play them against each other. Putting the list and the pricing floor on one table costs nothing in acquisitions.

Any business with several sales teams quoting the same customer separately is paying for the privilege of bidding against itself.

**Takeaway** · Leverage comes from having one counter, not more assets - consolidating scattered rights into a single sales desk buys a seat at the pricing table without buying scale.
