# Fifty-One/Forty-Nine Already Appeared in Rhett's 2024 Copyright Line

**What happened** · ADA Central Europe, a subsidiary of Warner Music Group, has signed an exclusive global digital distribution agreement with Swiss music label Sound Distribution. The partnership enables artists to own their labels and design revenue distribution structures directly.

- **Sound Distribution** · Swiss record label that signed a global digital distribution deal with Warner Music's ADA Central Europe on October 5, 2026. Recently transitioned from DJ format to independent label. Swiss label that signed an exclusive worldwide digital distribution deal with ADA Central Europe.
- **Thomas Rhett** · American country singer known for hit songs including Marry Me and Die a Happy Man. Country singer who set up Fifty-One/Forty-Nine Records with Blue Highway.
- **Tonherd** · An Austrian music label, recording studio, and publishing company based in Vienna. Known for work with artists like Josh. and Ina Regen. Vienna label that signed the same exclusive distribution deal with ADA Central Europe.

**Why this one** · What majors concede to artists is shifting from contract terms to ownership of the label entity itself. HYBE America and Warner's ADA using the same structure in the same week shows where a distributor's revenue point is settling.

**What is showing** · Within three months ADA Central Europe signed Berlin's AIM Music (July), Vienna's Tonherd (Aug-Sep) and Switzerland's Sound Distribution (Oct) on the same 'founder keeps the label, major takes distribution' template, while in the same week HYBE's Blue Highway launched Thomas Rhett's own imprint and HARDY's Mockingbird Records signed its first act.

---

Warner Music's distribution arm ADA Central Europe signed an exclusive worldwide digital distribution deal with the Swiss label Sound Distribution on October 5, [Music Business Worldwide](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi6AFBVV95cUxNQnM3T0p6OXQ5Z0xEOFpVV1MyVnZaN0NTRDVfT25zMGpKQlBMQmJHN3h2dGpnNzh6V044cENkODZIdzlET0tST3dDWi1tNkk3c1BxUDdNNm5wSzBXbW51UGNTRkpkMXNPa0dLcDY1SEVkTUVNMmI4RmxLc3Y2bHJFS1k0WW90eVhvZWdSSmtpOXFEamRhR3RyczUtNEhYSU5VTm9zZW5ReGN5SmFtVXFPb3RFd1BZN1FNWi1sN0UzWG1LR0czbW9ia1dPUldIY0J4N0Q5YnphaDZCR1VHc1Y4bE1fM0NHRFg5?oc=5) reported in 2026. A distributor is the company that delivers recordings to services like Spotify and Apple Music and counts the money that comes back. The first release under the deal, a debut single by Inéz & Pacoussa, came out three days earlier on October 2, according to [HITS Daily Double](https://www.hitsdailydouble.com/news/business/ada-central-europe-sound-distribution-partnership-2026). The same week in North America, the country singer Thomas Rhett set up Fifty-One/Forty-Nine Records with Blue Highway, a label under HYBE America, [Variety](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMinAFBVV95cUxPRFZUWGpTcFduMWZpRVRpbExxM29xVDNmR0dvWXlyTWZZTUp2aVhrMnk4UmhjblNDQlN1b1AteUpIZVJERjhvZUlILS1EYldBVHIyaUtralUyRkg0STZsTUtuQXJVRTMxZDNITVVzRjRqc0lWaHNob0FHOXNmQVRTdzlPUEJiTDcyN2E3NGoyTUs0RkVGeDZTWUNSZk8?oc=5) reported in 2026.

The two deals put the major company in the same seat. Under the old arrangement, a major label paid to record and promote an artist and in exchange kept the masters, the ownership of the recordings themselves, often for decades. In these two deals the ownership stays with the founders and the artist, and the major supplies distribution, accounting and audience data for a fee.

This is not a one-off. ADA Central Europe signed the same kind of exclusive deal with the Berlin startup label AIM Music on July 2, 2026, and with Vienna's Tonherd on August 27, Music Business Worldwide reported in 2026. Three months, three repetitions. Leaving ownership on the other side of the table has become a listed feature of the product rather than a concession.

The feature is not open to everyone. The name Fifty-One/Forty-Nine already appeared in the copyright line of Rhett's 2024 album "About a Woman," which was exclusively licensed to Valory Music Co., Music Business Worldwide confirmed in 2026. The company had been running under someone else's licence for two years. The ownership announced this month is an existing working relationship with a new label on it.

The arithmetic explains the shift. Owning recordings means that when a release fails, the money spent is simply gone. Running distribution and accounting systems means the cost per release falls as the number of releases rises. So the intermediary sheds the asset that carries variable loss and moves to a recurring fee that scales. The artist keeps all of the upside and also all of the downside. Rhett's equity split, financial terms and whether his earlier catalogue moves across were not disclosed.

That trade only closes for someone who arrives with an audience already built and enough capital to survive a quiet year. Both sides gain now because streaming has made release costs small and data cheap to serve, while hit rates stayed unpredictable. It would stop working the moment a wave of artist-owned labels runs out of cash between records, because the fee layer earns nothing from catalogues that stop releasing.

Any industry that has turned ownership of inventory into a licensing fee, from hotel chains to franchised restaurants, is watching the same transfer: the brand keeps the system, the operator keeps the risk.

**In short** · ADA Central Europe has signed three of these deals in three months, collecting a fee for delivery, accounting and data while each label keeps its recordings.
