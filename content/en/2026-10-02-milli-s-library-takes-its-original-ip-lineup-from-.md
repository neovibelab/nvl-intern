# Milli's Library Takes Its Original IP Lineup From 44 to 124

**What happened** · Millie's Sejae has expanded its original IP lineup from 44 to 124 titles and is diversifying into webtoon and web novel platforms through KT Media's value chain. The platform aims to establish a vertically integrated structure from discovering new writers to video adaptation and overseas expansion, targeting 5-10% market share within three years.

- **D&C Media** · South Korean webtoon and web novel publisher established in 2012. A web novel publisher that lists author supply as its core competitive strength.
- **Lee Myung-woo** · Executive leading web novel and webtoon business at Millie's Bookstore, a KT subsidiary. Oversees expansion from e-book subscriptions to web content. Head of Milli's Library story business division who explains the subscription model's advantage for unknown writers.

**Why this one** · When a subscription platform that merely resells others' works starts owning source IP, its position in the revenue split changes. For music streamers and distributors weighing whether to absorb labels, this is a live case study in cost and payback period.

---

Milli's Library (Millies Library (밀리의서재)), a South Korean e-book subscription service owned by the telecom group KT, said at a press briefing on October 1, 2026 that it will expand its original IP lineup from 44 titles this year to 124 next year. IP here means a story the company owns outright rather than licenses, which is what lets it sell the same work again as a webtoon, a web novel, a foreign-language edition or a drama. The plan links five stages, from finding writers to production, distribution and screen adaptation, and positions the company as the source-story supplier inside KT's media businesses. [Edaily (이데일리)](https://edaily.co.kr/News/Read?mediaCodeNo=257&newsId=05113526645608656) reported a target of 5 to 10 percent of the webtoon and web novel market within three years, while ZDNet Korea reported 10 percent within three years and 20 percent later. The two figures do not match.

The lineup count matters less than how the writers get there. In webtoons and web novels, the scarce thing is not shelf space but people who can draw and write at serial pace. D&C Media, a Korean publisher of web novels, listed securing authors as its core competitive strength in its 2025 business report, and noted in the same document that platforms run new-release banner promotion on their own initiative to feed their own traffic, so publishers pay no separate marketing cost. Author supply is the contest, and the promotion a writer receives rides on whoever owns the audience.

A latecomer cannot win that contest with advances, the upfront cash a platform pays a writer before any reader arrives, because Naver and Kakao have more of it. So Milli is paying in distribution instead. Lee Myung-woo (Lee Myeongu (이명우)), head of its story business division, told [Digital Daily](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026100111003454199) that a subscription model favors unknown writers, because a monthly flat fee removes the moment where a reader decides whether an unfamiliar name is worth paying for episode by episode. On top of that, a writer who premieres on Milli may then re-release the same work on Naver or Kakao. No exclusivity is demanded. Lee told ZDNet Korea that this parallel listing is eroding the exclusive-content model, and that the erosion works in Milli's favor.

The trade only holds if subscription exposure is worth as much as cash. The company's own figures, as of August, are 9.69 million cumulative shelf-adds for web novels and webtoons and more than 600 partnership titles. A shelf-add is a save, not a read, and the 600 are other people's works. Lee conceded to EBN that recognition is still at the level of subscribers noticing that webtoons exist inside the app.

Writers gain reach without surrendering ownership, Milli gains a catalogue without a cash war, and the moment subscribers stop walking from the e-book screen to the comics tab, the writers go back to whoever pays upfront. Any business buying scarce talent with access rather than money is making the same bet.

**Takeaway** · Where creators are the bottleneck, a late-entrant platform loses any advance-payment war against incumbents, so it instead designs non-cash compensation built into its own distribution-guaranteed exposure inside a flat-fee subscriber base, permission to re-distribute on rival platforms, no exclusivity lock-to flip supply. This only works once its subscriber base exceeds the exposure creators expect elsewhere.
